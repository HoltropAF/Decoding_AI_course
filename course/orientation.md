# Welcome to the course

AI tools have arrived in our lab the way they arrive everywhere: one person
at a time. Some of us use ChatGPT or Copilot every day, some tried it once
and got a confidently wrong answer, and most of us aren't quite sure what we
may paste into a chat window. This course is our way of sorting that out
together. Not to turn anyone into an AI developer, but so that all of us can
**use these tools well**: understand roughly how they work, know what
they're good and bad at, join the conversations about them with more than a
gut feeling, and get better at asking them for what we actually need.

The route is simple. **Module 1** explains what AI is and how it works, which
tools there are, which of our data may go where, what you can use it for,
and the big debates around it. **Module 2** is about prompting: how to ask
so you get reliable, checkable answers. **Modules 3 to 5** show how to get
more out of the tools: making them follow your rules, letting them look
things up, and letting them do small jobs for you. They have technical parts
that you're welcome to skim. **Module 6** is a two-day hackathon in small
teams, where we put it all together, with a track that needs no programming
at all. A comfortable pace is one module every week or two, alone or in a
small group session; groups are more fun, because you can both compare your
results and discuss any uncertainties ont he spot.

Every module works the same way. You start with the **chapter**, which reads
like a chapter of a friendly textbook: an opening story, parts that each
end with a short recap, little "pause and think" questions with the answer
hidden until you click, and a summary at the end. After that come an
optional **exercise** (always with a no-code version), a **self-check** with
a small experiment, and, linked at the end of each chapter, **slides** if
you'd like a quick recap. Under
**Wrap-up** you tick off what you've done and read back and add to your
notes. Stuck on a word? Hover over anything with a dotted underline, or open
the **Dictionary**. Prefer listening? Press **Read aloud**.

There's one ground rule we ask everyone to keep from day one: **think before
you paste**. Anything you type into a cloud AI tool leaves your computer and
goes to the company that runs it. Published papers and public datasets are
fine; unpublished results, drafts and anything from patients or donors are
not, unless your institution has approved a tool for that. All course
exercises use public or made-up data, so you never have to take that risk
here. Module 1 explains the details. And please check the privacy settings
of any AI account you already use this week: whether your chats may be used
for training, and how long they are kept.

To get ready, you need very little: a browser, this dashboard, and access
to a chat assistant such as ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Microsoft Copilot
(check with IT which ones your work account may use). For the exercises
that run a model on your own computer, install **Ollama** from
[ollama.com](https://ollama.com) and download a model with
`ollama pull llama3.1:8b`, ideally before the first session, because it's
about 5 GB. If you'd like to run the course scripts too, you'll need Python;
the setup steps are in the course's README on GitHub, and someone in the
group is always happy to help. If you encounter problems, ask your AI to 
explain to you what is going on and how you can work around your problems.

Finally: this course was written with a lot of help from AI and mostly checked 
by people, which means it can still contain mistakes. If something looks off,
use **Report a problem** in the menu. There are no exams and no grades, and
there are no stupid questions. Grab a coffee (and a snack), and start with 
Module 1.
